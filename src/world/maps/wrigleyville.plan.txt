Wrigleyville — ruined Chicago, north side

This file IS the map. One character per tile, 144 x 144. Edit it freely; the game reloads it.

  EDITING
    Easiest: `npm run dev`, then open /editor.html. It paints with the mouse, shows the real
    colours, regenerates the right-hand side as you draw, and Ctrl+S writes this file.
    Pencil / Line / Rect / Fill / Pick, Ctrl+Z to take back a whole stroke.

    By hand in a text editor also works, with two warnings. Turn on overtype/replace first, or
    every character you insert shifts the rest of the row and the loader will refuse to start —
    VS Code needs the "Overtype" extension for that. And the game draws square cells while an
    editor's are about 0.6 as wide as they are tall, so the map looks stretched; roughly
    `"editor.fontSize": 13, "editor.lineHeight": 8` squares it up.

    Every row must be exactly the same length. The loader names the line if one isn't.

  DRAWN EXACTLY AS YOU PUT THEM
    =  arterial roadway — the busy streets. More people, more patrols, more trouble.
    .  ordinary roadway
    '  alley — quiet, and things live down them
    ,  kerb / pavement
    #  brick wall          *  impassable ruin      :  rubble you can cross
    ~  water (impassable)  %  swamp                "  weeds
    T  thicket             g  kept grass           _  floor
    +  door                >  stairs down          <  stairs up

  LEFT TO THE GENERATOR  (any shape — it fills whatever outline you draw)
    B  a block of standing buildings, subdivided into footprints
    C  a block that came down — cave, winding passages through solid ruin
    ?  let the damage field decide between the two

  PLACES
    There are no landmark characters any more — one per landmark did not scale, and every letter
    spent on one was a letter the terrain could not have. Named places live in
    `src/world/places/wrigleyvillePlaces.ts` with their coordinates, and they do three things:
    say where a place's people and doors go, give the player a location instead of a pair of
    numbers, and protect whatever is drawn inside them from the weather passes.
    So the ball park and the station below are drawn, tile by tile, and yours to redraw.
    The editor outlines them and names them, so you can see where they think they are.

  NOTES
    Clark Street is the diagonal, as it is in life, cutting across the grid and leaving the
    odd triangular lot behind it. Everything else is the orthogonal north-side grid.
    The ~ along the park's west and north flanks is the Vigil's moat; the gates onto Addison
    are deliberately left dry.
    This street layout is a first draft by somebody whose Chicago is general rather than
    street-level. Moving things is the point of the file.
